# Chapter 20 — Adding a Synthesizer Pattern

A **motif** is a recognizable small musical idea; a **sequence** places its events over time. The four-note motif uses a higher register, short notes, repeated rhythm, and an octave-lifted return. It is structured `Event` data, so notes, octave (`±12`), start, duration, velocity, and repetition are independently editable.

Compare `04-add-harmony.wav` with `05-add-synth.wav`; inspect the synth row. Try one change at a time and regenerate. A simple sine-plus-harmonic timbre keeps attention on musical function rather than preset collecting.

## Listening lab
**Objective:** hear foreground created by register and rhythmic articulation. **Conditions:** with/without synth, otherwise identical. **Listen for:** whether the motif attracts attention and whether its rests preserve clarity. **Observations:** connect heard notes to event starts. **Reflection:** does the motif support focused listening? **Limitations:** “foreground” is contextual, not an amplitude-only measurement.

Part V will explain oscillators, envelopes, filters, and how synthesizers generate timbre. Part VI examines the samples in these WAV files; Part VIII connects event data to MIDI. Here the synthesizer remains a musical tool.
