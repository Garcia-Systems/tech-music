# Chapter 21 — Texture, Space, and Layers

A **layer** is one organized part; **texture** describes their combined relationships; **density** concerns how much occurs; **register** concerns where pitches sit. Foreground attracts attention, background supports it, and silence is usable musical space. Addition is not automatically improvement: subtraction can clarify purpose.

Create **A—too sparse** from kick only, **B—balanced** from drums+bass+chords, and **C—overcrowded** by duplicating the synth in two octaves. These labels are experimental prompts, not verdicts. Evaluate clarity, density, distraction, interest, and purpose; another listener may reasonably choose differently.

## Listening lab
**Objective:** compare density. **Conditions:** normalize each version and preserve duration. **Listen for:** masking-like competition, register separation, and rests. **Observations:** count active layers and separately describe experience. **Reflection:** does removing a layer improve the piece? **Limitations:** layer count cannot measure musical quality.

A failing function is an **implementation bug**; a wrong event value is a **data bug**; a signal sent to the wrong destination is a **routing bug**; an overcrowded but correctly rendered score may be a **production decision**. Tests can confirm valid bounds, not that B is universally best.
