# Learning path and reader paths

The book is one journey: **Listen → Compose → Produce → Understand Digital
Audio → Process Sound → Represent Musical Events → Understand Software
Architecture → Program Music Systems → Connect Hardware and Software → Explore
Generative and Adaptive Music**.

| Stage | Parts | Purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 — Music | I–III | Listen deliberately, learn musical vocabulary, and construct a track. |
| 2 — Music software | IV–V | Model a DAW and learn synthesis as controlled signal generation. |
| 3 — Audio engineering | VI–VII | Represent digital audio, then transform and measure signals. |
| 4 — Musical data | VIII | Represent notes, control, time, and event ordering. |
| 5 — Computer science | IX | Connect buffers, callbacks, concurrency, graphs, persistence, and testing. |
| 6 — Building systems | X | Assemble the earlier ideas into a small music application. |
| 7 — Full workstation | XI | Trace the whole path through computers, interfaces, drivers, and monitoring. |
| 8 — Generative future | XII | Build deterministic, probabilistic, adaptive, and human-directed systems. |

Part indexes explain their local progression and capstones. A reader may revisit
theory rather than reading strictly linearly, but Parts VI–IX supply vocabulary
assumed by the later systems work.

## Flexible reader paths

- **Programmer new to music:** begin with Parts I–III; do not skip the listening labs.
- **Musician new to programming:** read in order and allow extra time for Parts VI, VIII, IX, and X.
- **Producer interested in engineering:** begin with Parts III–VII, then use VIII–XI as needed.
- **Software engineer interested in audio:** focus on Parts VI–X, using II and V as musical context.
- **Generative-music reader:** complete at least Parts II, V–VIII, and X before XII.

These are suggestions, not prerequisites. Use the [master TOC](../book/README.md)
and [glossary](glossary.md) to move between a concept and its deeper treatment.
